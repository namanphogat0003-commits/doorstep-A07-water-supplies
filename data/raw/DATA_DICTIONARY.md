# Doorstep — Track A Simulated Dataset

**Track A · "Service as a Service"** · BTech 7th Sem AI & ML Thematic Assessment

This is the starter dataset for all twenty Track A modules (A01–A20). It simulates two
years of operations (1 Jan 2024 – 31 Dec 2025) for **Doorstep**, the fictional mobile
car-wash company described in your guidebooks. Every file is internally consistent: the
same booking, crew and customer IDs join cleanly across files, so your module's output can
feed the next team's input exactly as the guidebooks promise.

> **This data is simulated.** It was generated by `generate_doorstep.py` (included) with a
> fixed random seed, so it is reproducible and contains no real personal data. It is *not*
> scraped or real operational data — say so in your paper, and cite it as
> "Doorstep simulated dataset, Track A, generated <date>". If your module also uses a public
> dataset (A14 car images, A15 reviews, A19 benchmark problems), cite that separately.

- `duplicate` — a small batch of erroneously double-logged rows on **one** day (a planted data glitch for A17). They have no `jobs_done` row and no revenue. A11/A09 etc. should filter these out (`status != 'duplicate'`); A17 should learn to tell this glitch apart from a *real* surge.

## weather.csv & holidays.csv & vehicles.csv

**weather.csv** — daily: `date`, `temp_c`, `rain_mm`, `condition`
(`clear`/`rain`/`heavy_rain`/`heatwave`/`cold`). Monsoon (~Jul–mid-Sep) depresses demand.

**holidays.csv** — `date`, `name`, `demand_multiplier`. >1 = pre-festival wash rush
(Diwali eve, Holi eve); <1 = the holiday itself. Feeds A02 features and A17 context.

**vehicles.csv** — `vehicle_type`, `capacity_litres`, `purchase_cost_inr`,
`fuel_cost_per_km`, `co2_g_per_km`, `crew_size`. For A07 tank planning and A12 fleet trade-offs.

**daily_summary.csv** — convenience roll-up: `date`, `total_bookings`, `no_shows`,
`revenue`, weather, `is_holiday`, `dow`. A02 and A17 can start here, but should be able to
rebuild it from `bookings.csv`.

## Structure deliberately baked in (so your analysis has something to find)

- **Weekly cycle:** Saturday busiest, midweek quietest — the reason A03 (pricing) exists.
- **Growth trend:** 2025 is ~20% busier than 2024 — A02 must not assume a flat mean.
- **Seasonality + weather:** spring/autumn peaks; monsoon and peak-summer dips.
- **Festival spikes/dips:** pre-Diwali/Holi rush, holiday-day slumps.
- **No-show imbalance:** ~3.3% — A11's whole point. Accuracy is a trap here.
- **Skill → rating signal:** higher-skilled crews earn higher ratings (A08).
- **New joiners:** 4 crews start late and, under pure-efficiency allocation, get the worse jobs — the inequality A20 measures.
- **Two planted anomalies (A17):** a *real* demand surge (12 May 2025, heatwave) and a *data glitch* (3 Sep 2024, duplicated rows). Telling them apart is the module.
- **Honest gaps:** ~0.8% of prices missing, some customers with no ratings. Handle them; don't pretend they aren't there.

`anomaly_key.json` holds the ground truth for the two planted A17 events. **Keep it for
grading — don't hand it to the A17 team**, who are meant to discover them.
